Free up storage on a Mac

Your Mac says the disk is almost full, and it's not clear what you can safely delete.

How to do it in Finder

macOS has its own overview for this:

  1. Open Apple menu › System Settings › General › Storage.
  2. A bar at the top shows what uses the space. Below are recommendations, such as “Empty Trash Automatically”.
  3. Under “Documents” you'll find large files, downloads and unsupported apps, and can delete them.

In Finder, list view with “Calculate all sizes” (⌘J) also helps you find large folders. macOS doesn't show duplicate files.

How to do it with Foldair

Foldair offers two helpers:

  1. Storage (⌥⌘S): shows a bar with the size of every folder, so you quickly see where the space goes.
  2. Storage agent (in the agent, “Storage” card): “Check now” looks for duplicate videos, pictures and files, backups and copies, caches and build leftovers, old installers and archives, and large files that haven't been opened for a long time. Each finding is rated in three levels: can go, probably not needed, or check it yourself.

The agent never deletes anything on its own. It shows you where things are, and only on your click does something go to the Trash. If you like, it monitors the disk and warns you when free space drops below a limit (for example 50 GB).

Frequently asked questions

Does Foldair delete files on its own?

No. The storage agent only makes suggestions. What you click goes to the Trash, and you can restore it from there.

What can I safely delete on a Mac?

Usually old installers (.dmg, .pkg), duplicate files and caches that apps recreate. For your own documents and photos you should decide yourself – Foldair marks such findings as ones to check yourself.

Is the storage agent free?

The storage view with the bars stays free. The storage agent is part of the agent: you can try it free for 14 days, after that it needs a license (from €19.99, no subscription).
